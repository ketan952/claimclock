# ClaimClock competitor check

Reviewed 2026-10-10. This is a public-page and Google Play listing review, not a test of every logged-in product or installed app. The request asks about releases “since Oct 2026”; the review covers information available through October 10 only. No later release is represented as verified.

## Finding and product decision

No live statutory discharge countdown or NL-48 comparison was found in the inspected public pages/listings. This does **not** establish that none exists. ClaimBhai, UnDeny and ClaimSeva could not be fully inspected. Avoid “first,” “only,” and categorical competitor-absence claims.

ClaimClock's most defensible emphasis is a written acknowledgement of the hospital's submission time and request ID, a local evidence timeline with a visible statutory clock, attributable delay-charge documentation, and insurer-published NL-48 data with its bucket limitations clearly displayed. Generic appeal drafting substantially overlaps existing products. ClaimRights already advertises a deduction-challenge letter and a bonus timeline tracker; do not claim that competitors lack all timeline tools.

## Validation limitations

The accompanying user evidence is a convenience sample of public self-reports, with explicit deduplication and date/scope flags. It is useful for workflow design, not insurer rankings or estimates of legal noncompliance. A hospital's medical discharge time, its billing/submission time, the TPA's receipt time and final authorization time are different events. Reports without those timestamps cannot establish a statutory breach. Reimbursement settlement delays must not be counted as breaches of the three-hour discharge-authorization window.
