# ClaimClock v2 priorities

This is a proposed roadmap, not a list of shipped features or promised dates. Keep the hospital clock, source-linked Index and manual complaint tools usable without login, a paid API or an optional server. The user always reviews and files their own complaint.

Unresolved release requirements remain release work: dependency-license policy, authorized deployment, live-browser QA, optional-provider testing and any missing acceptance checks must not be relabeled as v2. See [RESUME.md](../RESUME.md) for the current status.

| Priority | Work | Why it comes next | Acceptance evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| P0 | Independently verify privacy and data handling before enabling optional reports or AI | Health documents and exact timestamps deserve stronger assurance than a design statement | Browser/network tests prove documents stay local; every AI request has a redacted preview and explicit consent; processor retention/settings reviewed; operator privacy contact published; deletion, quota failure, retention jobs and breach runbook exercised. Review applicable data-protection requirements without claiming blanket compliance. |
| P0 | Strengthen verification of rules, contacts and data changes | A stale destination or overbroad rule can make an escalation less useful | Changed primary-source content creates a human review task; HTTP success alone never advances verification dates. Preserve old values, dates and evidence; recheck every published legal claim and current filing portal. |
| P1 | Expand and independently review NL-48 coverage | Current numerical coverage is 17 of 32 roster insurers, with partial filings and one older period | Prioritize missing Star Health, Care, United India and Royal Sundaram sources; reviewed OCR for National's scanned PDFs; resolve Galaxy's unit conflict with a clear official source; finish partial TPA tables. Every added row has its period, provider, source page and exact unit checked. Never fill missing data with estimates. |
| P1 | Validate patient-report publication and abuse controls before promoting comparisons | Voluntary reports are neither representative nor independently verified; timestamps can reveal people | Test suppression, closed-month publication, deletion-related suppression, repeated-query inference and duplicate/spam submissions. Publish sample sizes and reporting periods. No small cells, hospital names or raw timelines; avoid comparing mismatched populations or declaring an insurer breached a rule. |
| P1 | WhatsApp entry point | A family may reach the tool from a forwarded message while standing at the desk | A free share/deep link opens the local clock with no personal data in its URL. Messages remain user-controlled drafts; no automatic sending, credentials or paid WhatsApp Business API dependency. Verify on Android and iPhone. |
| P1 | More Indian languages and accessibility checks | A correct message must also be understandable under stress | Start with languages selected through user research. Native speakers check legal meaning, calm death-case wording and everyday reading level. Test screen readers, large text, low literacy and low bandwidth. Preserve original source quotes with clearly marked translations. |
| P2 | Embeddable clock/Index widget for advisors, including services such as Beshak or Ditto | Advisors can direct people to the source and timestamp message without another account | A small static embed keeps source dates and limitations visible. No cross-site tracking, shared patient storage or silent data transfer to the embedding site. Partners receive no access to a family's timeline by default. No partnership or endorsement is implied. |
| P2 | Hospital-desk mode | Staff and families need a shared record of what was sent, received and queried | A minimal receipt/handoff view distinguishes hospital submission from insurer receipt and records corrections visibly. Test shift handover and printable acknowledgements. Do not create a shared patient database or desk-wide document access without a separate consent, access-control and retention design. |
| P2 | Policy-wording and schedule extraction for sub-limits and room-rent review | Coverage depends on the actual policy version, schedule and endorsements | Local extraction links every proposed clause to its original page and asks the user to confirm it. Missing/ambiguous text stays unknown. Test renewal versions, riders, co-pay, room entitlement and package terms. No blanket claim that every proportional deduction or consumable is recoverable; optional AI cannot override verified rules or user corrections. |
| P3 | State consumer-commission filing kits | A complete evidence packet can help after insurer escalation | Verify state/jurisdiction-specific forms, fees, addresses and attachment requirements against primary sources. Link to [e-Jagriti](https://e-jagriti.gov.in/) rather than stale e-Daakhil instructions. Include a forum/status check and explain that reminder dates do not determine limitation. The claimant reviews, signs and submits; the app never files automatically. |

Build each feature around a concrete user test: someone can identify the next action, understand what data leaves their device, and complete that action without buying a service. Measure success through separately consented minimal counts or voluntary usability sessions, not health-document analytics.

For policy and escalation work, use the [verified rule register](../data/rules.json), [regulatory corrections](../research/regulatory-verification.md), [IRDAI's Health Master Circular](https://irdai.gov.in/document-detail?documentId=4942918), [IRDAI grievance guidance](https://irdai.gov.in/grievance-redressal-mechanism1) and [Ombudsman eligibility guidance](https://www.cioins.co.in/Faqs). Reverify them when implementing each release. Privacy work starts from the [operations document](privacy-operations.md) and the [official DPDP rules and commencement collection](https://www.meity.gov.in/documents/act-and-policies/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-gDOxUjMtQWa?pageTitle=Digital-Personal-Data-Protection-Rules-2025), with implementation evidence and applicable-rule review.
