## Draft 1 — The Index: what insurers report and what patients report

How long does cashless approval take? Insurers already publish part of the answer, scattered across PDFs.

ClaimClock brings 470 published timing distributions from 30 insurers into one source-linked dataset. Each row keeps the TPA, reporting period, individual/group policy category and original percentages. The coverage register records gaps across 32 insurers.

One limit matters: the **2–6 hour bucket cannot tell us whether the three-hour discharge deadline was met**. This is not a league table. Most included data is FY2025-26; one insurer's latest located filing is FY2024-25. Missing data is shown as missing.

The intended comparison is “what insurers report” alongside “what patients report.” There are no patient results to announce in this draft. Any future patient figures must be voluntary, clearly labeled and shown only when privacy thresholds are met.

## Draft 2 — The one message to send before you wait

“We sent the request” is a useful start. A written time and request ID are more useful when the wait grows.

Send this to the hospital insurance desk or TPA:

> Please confirm in writing when the hospital sent my cashless request, when the insurer/TPA received it, and the request ID. Please tell me its current status and whether anything is pending.

Keep the reply. If the desk only knows the sending time, record that uncertainty too.

IRDAI's circular sets a one-hour initial cashless decision window and a three-hour final discharge authorization window, triggered by receipt of the relevant request. The time you were told it was sent may differ from the insurer's receipt time. [Primary source: paragraphs 15(b) and 16(a)](https://irdai.gov.in/document-detail?documentId=4942918).

ClaimClock helps you record those times, keep a timeline and prepare your own escalation message. Its contact directory contains 49 source-verified records for 32 insurers and 17 TPAs. You review and send; ClaimClock does not file or contact anyone for you.

## Draft 3 — You may be owed the extra room rent

Did a cashless discharge delay add another room or nursing charge to your bill?

IRDAI's Health Insurance Master Circular says that when final authorization is delayed beyond three hours, additional hospital charges caused by that delay are borne by the insurer from its shareholder fund. The three-hour window starts when the insurer receives the hospital's discharge authorization request. [Primary source: paragraphs 16(a)–(b)](https://irdai.gov.in/document-detail?documentId=4942918).

Keep three things:

1. The insurer/TPA receipt time and final decision time, with any written acknowledgements.
2. The itemized extra charge and payment receipt.
3. The hospital's explanation of how the delay caused that charge.

Ask the insurer to pay the documented delay-related amount, or give its written reasons. This rule is not a fixed payment for every hour, and it does not make every unpaid bill item recoverable.

ClaimClock helps turn your timeline and extra charges into a recovery request you can review and send yourself. It cannot promise payment.
